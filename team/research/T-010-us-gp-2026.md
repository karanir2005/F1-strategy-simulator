## 0. TL;DR for T-011

1. **Pecking order has moved a lot since 2025**: Mercedes took 11 of 17 2026 wins (Antonelli 8, Russell 3). Mercedes' share of race points went 17.5% (2025) to 29.9%; McLaren fell 32.0% to 16.6%. 2025 car pace is useless as a prior. Red Bull has surged recently: Verstappen won the last two (Sepang, Singapore) from pole.
2. **Grid matters, but not as much as the pole numbers look.** 2026: pole converted 12/17 (71%), P2 4/17, no winner has ever started P3 or lower except Antonelli from P19 at Monza. Spearman(grid, finish) averages 0.65 per race (same as 2025: 0.65). Pooled 2022-26 pole win rate is 58% (n=109), so shrink the 2026 71%.
3. **Reliability is much worse in 2026**: 20.9% of driver-starts end in "Retired"/DNS vs 12.5% in 2025 (2022-24: 11-17%). Every contender had at least one DNF (Russell 3, Verstappen 4, Norris 4, Leclerc 3). Model DNF explicitly or the win/podium probabilities will be over-confident.
4. **US GP (conventional weekend)**: FP1 Fri 2026-10-23 12:30 CDT, FP2 16:00, FP3 Sat 12:30, **Qualifying Sat 2026-10-24 16:00 CDT (21:00 UTC)**, Race Sun 2026-10-25 15:00 CDT (20:00 UTC). Three practice sessions, so FP2/FP3 long runs exist; in 2025 it was a sprint weekend with one practice.
5. **COTA specifics** are weaker predictors than 2026 form: tyre wear is moderate-to-high, overtaking is "hard but possible", and every one of the last 4 races had at least one SC/VSC-like slow period. Winners came from P1/P2 in 11 of the last 13 races; the two exceptions (2023, 2024) were both messy.
6. **Calendar check answered**: round 16 "Bahrain Grand Prix" at "Kuala Lumpur" is real. See section 5.

## 2. COTA: track character

### 2.1 Tyres and strategy
- **2026 allocation: C2 hard / C3 medium / C4 soft** per a single Italian source ([Motorbox](https://www.motorbox.com/auto/sport/f1/news/f1-pirelli-gomme-gp-stati-uniti-messico-brasile-2026)). **[unverified]**: I could not find Pirelli's own press release, and that article is inconsistent about 2025. Treat as likely, confirm.
- 2025 used C1/C3/C4 (a deliberate non-consecutive gap to push 2-stops; Pirelli's model had the 2-stop a few seconds quicker than the 1-stop; [F1.com](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/pirelli-boss-isola-explains-jump-in-compounds-for-austin-and-mexico-city.6Vg3wKuNRHscMiwkH2Yqcw)). Teams largely ignored the C1 hard. 2024 was C2/C3/C4.

- No numeric degradation (s/lap) for COTA found in public text; lap-level data for 2022-2025 was not obtainable from my sandbox (see section 1 risk). **Request**: once the orchestrator's cache job lands 2025 US GP, T-009/T-011 can fit a per-track degradation prior.

### 2.2 Overtaking and track position
- Qualitative: abrasive surface, many high-speed corners and elevation changes make clean passes hard, with long straights giving slipstream opportunities ([The Race COTA ranking](https://the-race.com/formula-1/is-cota-a-modern-f1-classic-every-race-ranked) and secondary sources). **Moderate evidence only.**
- Quantitative from Jolpica: mean absolute grid-to-finish change for finishers, **COTA 2021-25: 2.97 places vs 2.86 all tracks 2022-25** (2026 so far: 2.75). COTA is essentially average for position movement; it is not a Monaco-style grid-locked track.
- COTA grid-vs-finish Spearman by year (field incl. DNFs, grid=pit lane as last): 2012 .63, 2013 .89, 2014 .66, 2015 .42, 2016 .36, 2017 .56, 2018 .21, 2019 .58, 2021 .75, 2022 .18, 2023 .14, 2024 .62, 2025 .75. **Mean 0.52 (2021-25: 0.49)** vs 0.65 for 2025/2026 fields overall. The low years are 2022 (grid penalties, two SCs), 2023 (Hamilton/Leclerc DSQ for plank wear), 2018 (Verstappen P18 to P2). Sample is tiny; do not hard-code a COTA-specific grid weight, but a track-level random effect on the grid coefficient is defensible.
- COTA winners' starting slots: 2012 P2, 2013 P1, 2014 P2, 2015 P2, 2016 P1, 2017 P1, 2018 P2, 2019 P1, 2021 P1, 2022 P2, 2023 P6, 2024 P4, 2025 P1. **Winner from P1/P2 in 11 of 13; pole won 6 of 13.**
- Podiums: 39 podium places over 13 races; podium-from-top-3-grid is typical (2025: grids 1, 2, 3).

### 2.3 Safety cars
- From the table above: 4 of 4 recent races (2022-25) had at least one slow-lap period (SC in 2022, 2024; VSC in 2025; VSC+neutralisation in 2023). Full SC in 2 of 4 verified, 3 of 4 if 2023 laps 33-36 were an SC (unverified). Earlier years (2013 lap-1 SC, 2016 VSC) found via search but I did not tabulate; **treat COTA P(at least one SC/VSC) as ~75-100% with wide uncertainty, P(full SC) ~50-65%**.
- 2026 baseline across the 10 pulled races (`data/processed/*_2026_track_status.csv`): full SC in **6/10** (China, Japan, Miami, Monaco, Britain, Belgium), VSC-only in the other 4 (Australia, Austria, Canada, Hungary); a red flag in 1/10 (Monaco). So every pulled 2026 race had at least one SC or VSC. This matches the COTA prior.

### 3.2 What plausibly carries over
- **Driver skill / consistency** (e.g. Verstappen's 2025 COTA record: 1.4 mean finish in 2021-25 incl. 4 wins; Norris 4.4). These are prior-level only; the new cars may change driver ranking (Verstappen has 4 DNFs in 2026 so far).
- **Track effects**: COTA SC/VSC rate, pit-lane time (~23-25 s), tyre-allocation pattern, grid-to-finish elasticity (section 2).
- **Driver-specific COTA form** (Verstappen 4 wins in 5 starts 2021-25; Hamilton 5 wins here 2012-2017) is mostly car-driven; I would give it little weight.
- **Rookie development**: Antonelli was a 2025 rookie and is now 8 wins deep. Anything keyed on "experience" from 2025 is stale.
- Rule continuity: **2025 vs 2026 differ in cars, PUs, tyre widths, DRS vs Overtake Mode, 768 kg** (T-001). Do not use 2025 lap times, only 2025 *outcomes at the track* with a down-weight.
- 2026 field changes: Cadillac (Bottas, Perez) new, Audi replaces Sauber, Hadjar at Red Bull, Lindblad at Racing Bulls, Colapinto at Alpine, 22 cars vs 20 in 2025 (rows per race 22 vs 20), so rank-based features must be normalised.

### 4.4 Practice long-run pace [limited evidence]
- Weissbock and Mills (2025, arXiv:2507.10966, ~7,800 driver-weekends over nearly two decades): **qualifying is the strongest predictor of race position, better than starting grid and practice results**; practice carries "some signal" and later sessions are more informative ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10966)). Only the abstract was readable to me; no coefficients retrieved.
- Practice-to-quali translation is noisy because teams switch programmes between sessions ([AWS ML blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/predicting-qualification-ranking-based-on-practice-session-performance-for-formula-1-grand-prix/)).
- I found **no published quantification of FP2 long-run pace vs race result**. Treat it as an untested feature. Practical caveat: long-run fuel loads and engine modes are unknown, so use it as a team-level adjustment (shrunk to the team mean), not a per-driver ranker.

### 4.5 Published approaches to podium prediction
- Hobbyist/open-source XGBoost or random-forest podium classifiers use grid, quali pace, recent form, constructor strength, reliability, weather; self-reported 75-80% test accuracy, ranking grid position first ([demo](https://f1-race-predictor-tl5m.onrender.com/), [project docs](https://www.mintlify.com/TomandJerry0811/Formula-1-prediction/resources/model-performance)). **Unverified; not peer reviewed; accuracy on a top-3 binary target with a ~15% base rate is not comparable to calibrated log-loss.**
- Academic: Weissbock and Mills above (ordinal logistic regression on qualifying/grid/practice); Mühlbauer (2010) examined only the top 8 starters in 2006-09 and overstated grid effect per the replication. Championship-level XGBoost: Den Hartog (2023) 93% accuracy (season-level, not applicable).
- No source found with a proper walk-forward calibration or betting-market comparison. T-011's walk-forward Brier/log-loss against grid/championship baselines is stricter than anything I found.

## 5. Calendar check: round 16 "Bahrain Grand Prix" in "Kuala Lumpur"

**Confirmed real.** The 2026 Bahrain GP (originally 2026-04-12 at Sakhir) and the Saudi Arabian GP were postponed/cancelled in March 2026 because of the Middle East (Iran-US) conflict, cutting the calendar from 24 to 22 races. FastF1's `Location="Kuala Lumpur"`, `EventDate=2026-10-04` is therefore correct (only the venue label is a stand-in; the circuit is Sepang). Race held 2026-10-04, won by Verstappen from pole; it was a wet-delayed start, with Russell stopping behind a late safety car, and Albon/Bottas not finishing ([PlanetF1](https://www.planetf1.com/news/f1-results-bahrain-grand-prix-malaysia-2026-race), [Crash.net](https://www.crash.net/f1/results/1106501/1/f1-bahrain-grand-prix-malaysia-full-race-results)). Consequences for modeling: Sepang is a new track for the dataset (tag it by circuit, not by "Bahrain"); do not join it to 2025 Bahrain/Sakhir track features. Wet race: a rain-affected sample. ## 7. Recommendations for T-011

**Features that earn their place (in rough priority):**
1. Starting grid (post-penalty) as a rank and/or per-race gap-to-pole, interacted with `event_format` (sprint weekends have different information). Use 2026 only to fit; shrink toward the 2022-26 pooled grid-to-finish curve above.
2. Team strength: exponentially weighted team race-pace or points over the last ~3-5 races (the 2026 pecking order shifted at least twice: McLaren mid-season, Red Bull in the last two). Last-3 average finish had rho 0.59 vs 0.65 for grid.
3. Driver effect: shrunk driver intercept within team (e.g. Antonelli/Russell gap).
4. **DNF/mechanical probability** per team and driver (2026 base rate 20.9% per start, big team differences 6-50%) as an explicit component, not noise.
5. Optional on Saturday: FP2/FP3 long-run pace gap to team mean (untested; evaluate only if the walk-forward data allow, 2026 FP laps may not be in cache).
6. Track covariates (low weight): COTA SC/VSC probability, tyre-stress class.

**Sanity checks / expected ranges for the final US GP table** (illustrative, to flag a broken model):
- The pole-sitter's win probability should land around 35-60%, not above 70% (2026 71% on n=17; pooled 58%; COTA 6/13 = 46%).
- P2 win ~15-25%; P3 5-10%; P4+ combined only a few percent (Antonelli-from-P19 style wins exist, so no zero probabilities).
- Podium probability sums to 3, win to 1. Expected number of top-3 grid starters on the podium ~1.9-2.2.
- Any driver with a nonzero chance of DNF (~12-20% for top teams) should show it; a car on the front row with P(win) of 90% is wrong.
- P(at least one SC/VSC) ~80%+ drives variance; the model's uncertainty should not collapse on pole.
- Do not use 2025 team pace; if 2025 is included at all, restrict it to track-level and driver-prior terms and verify it beats 2026-only in walk-forward (as T-011 already requires).

**Data hygiene**: use Jolpica or FastF1 `results` for grid *with penalties* (pit-lane start = 0); treat "Did not start" as separate from "Retired" (7 DNS in 17 races); 22 starters in 2026 vs 20 in 2025; Sepang is its own circuit; Barcelona (R7) and Madrid (R14) are different circuits.
