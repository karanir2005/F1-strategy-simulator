# T-049 Tyre wear model audit

Read-only audit, no shell. Everything below is from reading code/docstrings/handoffs, or my own arithmetic on shipped constants. "Not computed" marks things that need a run. Era: all model numbers are 2026 fits unless stated.

## Headline

The wear model is not "wrong" in a way that a bug fix repairs. It is **structurally unable to decide stops or splits**, and it was fitted on data whose identification is weak. Decisions currently come from (a) pit loss, (b) symmetry of equal linear slopes, (c) stint caps taken from what teams did.

### Why the optimizer gives H28/M28 (my arithmetic, verified against code)
Wear is linear, `age_coef` ~0.037 s/lap for all three compounds, offsets 0/0/0 (T-043 docstring), cliff placeholder not binding (docstring point 4: max_reliable_age ~50 > every cap). Cost of an n-lap stint = a*n(n+1)/2 + fuel, which is convex.
- With equal a and equal pace, the best split of 56 laps is symmetric: 28/28. Nothing in the model distinguishes M from H, so the split is a tie broken by fuel timing. Teams run M15-24 / H31-41 because M is less durable than H, which the model has no parameter for (T-043 handoff says the same: "M and H wear tied").
- Stop count: 1-stop 28/28 wear cost = 2*0.037*406 = 30.0 s. 2-stop 19/19/18 = 0.037*(190+190+171) = 20.4 s. Saving 9.6 s vs pit loss 24.2 s (`track_params.json`, austin). Break-even needs slope ~0.09 s/lap, about 2.5x the fitted 0.037. So 1 stop wins at any race length near 56 laps, as T-043 observed (1 stop in 30/34 races, no skill over "always 1 stop" 21/34).
- Revealed-preference check: with linear wear, the optimum satisfies a_M*n_M = a_H*n_H, so teams' M24/H32 implies a_M/a_H ~ 1.33, and S25/H31 implies a_S/a_H ~ 1.24 (ignoring pace offset and fuel timing). The model has 1.0/1.0 forced by the constraint.
- Current Austin caps (SOFT 29, MEDIUM 25, HARD 41; typical 26/24/36 in `track_params.json`) now force M<=24 so H>=32; that is what makes the plan look like teams. The caps, not the wear curve, are doing the work. Caps come from team behaviour, so agreement with teams is partly circular (T-043: stop-count match 13/14 with leaked caps vs 23/34 with clean caps).

## Ranked flaws

### 1. Wear is linear and identical in shape/size across compounds, so durability differences and stop economics cannot appear. CONFIRMED
- Evidence: above arithmetic; `predict_delta` + `fit_joint_model(constrain=True)`; wear 0.0369/0.0369/0.0364 (T-043 docstring). Quadratic was dropped because it did not win LORO on lap-time shape (docstring, T-002), but LORO on within-stint lap-time RMSE is the wrong yardstick for strategy: it rewards the flat average, not the end-of-life behaviour that sets stint length.
- Effect: stops and splits are decided by caps. Any new circuit without caps gets 1 stop and a symmetric split.
- Fix: stop asking lap-time regression alone to supply durability. Add a compound/C-number durability parameter estimated from the stint-length distribution (see #2, #4). Validate with eval_strategy.py LORO: stop-count (baseline 23/34; 2026 subset) and compound-set (7/14 in 2026) with caps REMOVED (or only the global cap) so the wear curve must do the work. Success = matches that do not rely on caps.

### 2. Right-censoring / selection of stints: observed laps come from tyres that were kept on. LIKELY (direction certain, size unmeasured)
- Mechanism: a team ends a stint when the tyre is (or is about to be) slow, or for SC/free-stop/undercut reasons. Laps at high age are therefore sampled from cars whose tyres were holding up, and laps removed by `clean_laps` (SC, restart laps, red-flag stints, in/out laps) are disproportionately the ones near stops. The regression sees a tyre that does not fall off because the ones that fell off were already changed.
- Evidence it matters here: (a) T-043 found "no measurable cliff: residual slope beyond typical stint length is 0.00 (2026), negative (2025)" (diag_hinge.py). That is exactly what truncation at the decision point produces, and says nothing about whether a cliff exists. (b) 44% of 2026 top-10 stops fall under SC/VSC (T-043), so many stints end for reasons unrelated to wear. (c) the p90 stint caps are the only thing bounding the optimizer.
- Not computed: how many laps exist at age > typical stint length per compound (diag_hinge counts these in its output; I did not run it).
- Does it explain "stops decided by caps"? Yes in part: the fitted slope is mostly an average over the early-mid part of stints, so it is small; the only information about the end of a stint is the stopping decision itself, which enters via caps.
- Fix: treat stint ends as data. Options, simplest first: (i) fit wear on laps up to the stint-length p50 only and compare slope vs full-range (if slopes differ, truncation matters); (ii) a stop-hazard / inverse-optimal model: choose per-compound curvature or durability so the optimizer's stint lengths reproduce the observed median stint (under green-only stops) in the training races, then test LORO. Caution from T-043: a single cliff constant tuned to team behaviour is not a measurement; only keep it if it beats caps-free baselines out of sample on held-out races. (iii) Exclude SC-triggered stops from the length distribution used to calibrate durability (they say nothing about wear).

### 3. Compound-ordering inversion is probably a phase-of-race/traffic confound, not (only) a property of 2026 tyres. LIKELY / speculative on the exact cause
- How wear is identified: one compound is typically run once per driver-race, so within the driver fixed effect, a compound's wear slope = its within-stint lap-time slope minus the race's fuel slope (age and lap_number move together inside a stint). Any thing that shapes lap time by race phase therefore lands on whichever compound is run in that phase. SOFT/MEDIUM are mostly first stints (grid-compressed traffic, cars in a train set pace by the car ahead, rubber laying down, heavy fuel) and HARD is mostly last. A traffic-limited first stint looks flat, hence "SOFT wears least" (free fit 0.025 < 0.036), and SOFT laps look no faster than HARD.
- Evidence for: free fit inverted in 99% of race-cluster bootstraps (docstring), robust to clean-air filters, and the same code gives the textbook order on 20 dry 2025 races. A pit-stop jump estimator says a fresh SOFT after a MEDIUM stint gains 0.5 s less than a fresh HARD (284 stops): that controls fuel/phase tightly, which cuts against a pure phase artefact and toward real 2026 tyre/energy management or a stop-selection effect (who stops for SOFT, and when). So I would not call the cause established.
- Evidence the forced ordering hides something: forcing it moved the SOFT offset by -0.25 s (T-043). That is the model telling you the wear and pace parameters are not separately identified (wear and offset trade off). Also the ordering constraint ties M=S wear=0.0369, which is the constraint boundary, not a measurement.
- Other candidates not checked: driver/team mix on each compound (driver FE handle this within driver, so low risk); fuel load at stint start (the per-race linear fuel slope shares one slope across compounds; a convex fuel/evolution effect would bias early-stint compounds); mixed used/new sets (clean_laps keeps used-set stints; start_age handled by tire_age).
- Fix: (a) add interaction-free phase controls: a flexible race-phase term per race (spline in lap_number or two-way driver x lap FE already tried: SOFT +0.10, MEDIUM +0.13 still inverted), so test instead a **stint-position control**: fit wear on laps 6+ of each stint only and on a 'gap to car ahead' covariate if `gap_*` columns exist; (b) use the within-driver paired contrast of stints of different compounds at the same lap_number range; (c) pool 2025 as a prior for the ordering and let 2026 move it (the T-043 note says 2025 as prior at weight 0.25-1 changes little in LORO metrics, so this is a low-regret option). Validate with the bootstrap used in calibrate_tire_compounds.py: ordering should stop flipping in resamples, and with eval_strategy.py compound-set match.

### 4. Pooling across tracks and C-numbers: compounds are treated as the same tyre everywhere; wear severity is confounded with C-number because there is one 2026 race per circuit. CONFIRMED (structure), effect likely moderate
- Evidence: the global wear is keyed on S/M/H label (fit_joint_model design: `age_SOFT/MEDIUM/HARD`). `compounds` in `track_params.json` show Austin HARD=C2, MEDIUM=C3, SOFT=C4; T-043 notes HARD is C1 elsewhere. Docstring (T-002) tried C-number keying only for the pace offset, not for wear. `fit_track_wear` fits one slope per (track, compound) with ridge tau=0.01 to the global, but with one 2026 race per circuit, track severity and C-number cannot be separated, and a new circuit (Las Vegas) falls back to the global slope. T-028 noted plain LORO is degenerate for this reason; the T-043 cross-era transfer (r about 0.6 across years) made no difference to stop counts.
- Also: the Austin params were hand-patched (C2/C3/C4) and `build_track_params.py` regenerates C1 (T-043); caps and compounds drift.
- Fix: replace label keying with a hierarchical model wear_{track,c} = severity_track * f(C-number), with f estimated from all 36 races (2025 for the C-number structure, scaled for the era), severity partial-pooled per track with a track-character prior (lap length, corner loads, surface). Validate by true LORO at the track level now that 2025 gives a second race per circuit: hold out a circuit's both seasons, predict stint-level slope; compare against global slope baseline (scorecard_ab / validate_scorecard --per-track).

### 5. Fuel and wear are only weakly separable; per-race fuel slope noise passes into wear. LIKELY
- Evidence: the live code is `fit_joint_model` (the legacy `fit_shared_fuel_coef` is superseded and underestimates fuel, -0.033 vs -0.054; it is no longer used, so no problem there). The joint fit identifies wear from age resetting at stops while lap_number does not, which is correct in principle. But inside a stint age and lap_number are collinear, so identification comes from between-stint variation in stop lap, which is small for the dominant 1-stop population and comes largely from SC-timed stops. Symptom: per-race "fuel" slopes range -0.003 to -0.100 s/lap (docstring), far wider than physical fuel burn can differ, because the slope also carries track evolution, tyre-management pace and field spreading. `fit_track_wear` then holds each race's own noisy slope fixed, so an error d in the fuel slope maps nearly one-for-one into that track's wear slope.
- Fuel physical prior: I did not find or verify a 2026 s/kg figure or fuel mass limit in sources here (unverified). Pre-2026 literature uses roughly 0.03 s/kg, but treat that as a prior only. The shipped lap-weighted -0.051 s/lap should be sanity-checked against (kg burned per lap) x (s per kg); not done.
- Fix: fix the fuel term at a physics value (per-track kg/lap x s/kg with an uncertainty), model track evolution separately (exponential-decay per race), and refit wear; report wear sensitivity to fuel +/-0.01 s/lap. If wear swings by a similar amount, the wear estimate is mostly the fuel assumption. Validate: eval_strategy.py plus within-stint LORO RMSE (should not worsen from 1.157 s).

### 6. Cliff is a placeholder that does not bind; "no cliff" is a data limit, not evidence. CONFIRMED
- Evidence: `COMPOUND_CLIFF_PENALTY_PLACEHOLDER` S1.0/M0.5/H0.2 s per lap; `predict_delta` applies it only beyond `min(max_reliable_age, cliff_age)`; max_reliable_age ~50 for all compounds and optimizer caps are <= p90 stint (36 for HARD at Austin). So the placeholder never enters the optimizer's result: confirmed by code reading. I did not check the current model's `cliff_age` values (not run); if `detect_cliff` returned a small age for any compound the boundary would move, so a quick print of `build_tire_model()` is worth doing.
- Shape alone cannot produce a stop without caps (arithmetic above), but a convex end-of-life term would. See #1/#2.

### 7. Other structural omissions. CONFIRMED present as omissions, effect unmeasured
- Track temperature: raw laps files carry what look like two temperature columns (Austin row: 36.8, 26.9; I could not confirm which is track vs air) and `data_loader.py` / `openf1_loader.py` reference them, but `tire_model.py` does not use any (grep: no temp in tire_model). Thermal degradation and warm-up are standard drivers of wear; a per-race temperature covariate on the wear slope is cheap to test.
- No tyre warm-up / out-lap shape (out-laps are dropped, so the first push lap after a stop carries no warm-up cost; in-lap/out-lap loss is lumped into pit loss).
- No traffic/dirty-air wear or time loss (single car; ROADMAP Phase 1, T-034).
- Driver/team wear differences are removed by driver FE on pace but not on wear slope (same slope for all).
- 2026 regs: Autosport (post-Austin) says the 2026 compounds are on the durable side and 2027 compounds should degrade more; the narrower 2026 tyres and energy-management driving are plausible reasons teams' lap times do not reflect tyre state, but I found no source quantifying it. Not verified numerically.

## Where the model is NOT fundamentally flawed
- Fuel/wear estimator form: joint fit with driver-race FE is the right structure (consistent with the practitioner approach below). The earlier "fuel slope disagrees" bug was fixed in T-002.
- Green-flag / restart / in-out lap cleaning is sound and measured.
- Constraint on pace offsets only (T-043) is the right call given the data; the inversion is not hidden by the shipped model (offsets 0).
- Pit loss is not the explanation (T-043: 22.4 s effective vs 23.2 s pit lane).
- A tyre model that yields about +-0.0 s/lap net lap-time trend inside a stint (fuel -0.051 vs wear +0.037 gives about -0.014 s/lap) is consistent with raw lap times; the lap-time fit is fine. The weakness is what it implies outside the observed stint lengths.

## Suggested order for the data scientist (after T-047 caps work)
1. Cheap diagnostics (no new model): wear slope on laps age<=p50 vs full; fuel +/-0.01 sensitivity; print cliff_age and max_reliable_age; count laps beyond typical stint length per compound.
2. Caps-free evaluation harness in eval_strategy.py (caps off or a single global cap) so any wear change is judged on whether it creates stops/compound splits itself.
3. Durability parameter per compound/C-number fitted from green-only stint-length distribution plus 2025, hierarchical by track (#1, #2, #4). Keep out of the shipped default unless it beats the baselines out of sample (stop-count 23/34, compound-set 7/14 in 2026).
4. Fix fuel physically and add track temperature (#5, #7).
