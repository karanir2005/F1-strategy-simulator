## Findings
1. MEDIUM - optimizer disagrees with real winners; cause is tire_model, not the optimizer. Winners ran 1-stop MEDIUM/HARD at most tracks (Australia M12/H46, China M10/H46, Japan M22/H31, Miami M26/H31, Madrid M14/H43, Abu Dhabi M23/H35, Qatar M7/M25/H25). Optimizer top plans are SOFT-heavy and often 2-stop with two SOFT stints (Monaco M14/S32/S32, Hungary M5/S33/S32, Mexico/Brazil/Qatar M5/S33/S33, Austria M21/S25/S25, Australia S24/M10/S24, Barcelona S21/S21/M24). Agreement: Belgium M18/S26 (exact), Austin M28/S28 vs M33/S23, Singapore M25/S37 vs S32/M30, Las Vegas H23/M27 vs M25/H25, Canada M31/S37 vs S31/M37. Counterfactuals (scratch runs, not committed): (a) the placeholder pace offsets do not explain it alone - zeroing them does nothing because the model's `_compound_offsets` is used, and (b) swapping in the *fitted* offsets (SOFT +0.38, MEDIUM +0.18 vs HARD, i.e. softs slower on the global fit) flips plans to HARD+SOFT/MEDIUM; (c) the fitted degradation is non-physical (age_coef SOFT 0.010 s/lap < MEDIUM 0.038 < HARD 0.050, likely stint-selection bias), which makes SOFT look like the best long-stint tyre. Using physical degradation (S .09, M .06, H .04) gives HARD+MEDIUM 1-stoppers close to real winners. So: placeholders (offset -0.5 SOFT, cliff) AND the fitted per-compound age_coef are both responsible. Owner: data-scientist (fit per-compound degradation controlling for stint selection; fit offsets/cliffs).
