## What I did
1. **Default track = next race.** `fetchPredictionRaces()` (`is_next`) runs alongside `fetchTracks()`; `trackIdForRaceName` in `lib/strategy-form.ts` matches the race name exactly against the track name before " (" ("Brazilian GP" vs "Brazilian GP (Brazil)"). Both sides come from the same Jolpica names, so all 2026 races map today (checked against the live lists). It does not use circuit names (they differ: "Autódromo José Carlos Pace" vs "Autodromo Jose Carlos Pace (Interlagos)"). If the lookup fails or nothing matches: old fallback (austin, then first track). Currently resolves to Brazil.
2. **T-033 "Compare this plan"** on the top plan and on every other listed plan. Adds a new manual strategy (stints filled, new sets, unique name like "2-stop H24-H24-M23"), resizes the other plans' last stint if the lap totals differ, scrolls to the section (smooth, instant under reduced motion), highlights the new card for 8 s, and shows a status line. Disabled with a tooltip when 20 strategies already exist.
3. **Ties.** A plan is "tied with best" if its mean gap is under `NOISE_FLOOR_S` (existing 5 s constant, from T-017) or its paired p10 gap is <= 0 (the API's `delta_to_best_p10_s`). Tied rows show `=1` and a "tied with best" badge, the heading says "Top plan" instead of "Optimal plan", and the gap chart labels them "tied". Live check: at Brazil, 6 two-stop plans are within 1.0 s of each other and read as ties.
4. **Stint timeline.** New `components/strategy/plan-timeline.tsx` wraps the existing `StintStrip` (already coloured with `--tyre-*` tokens) and adds pit-lap ticks (`L24`) positioned by lap. Used in the hero and the plan list.
5. **Plain-language summary** at the top of the results, built from the response: "Two stops, hard then hard then medium, pit around laps 24 and 48." plus the gap to the best plan with a different stop count (or "level with it, stop count is a judgement call" if that gap is also inside the noise).
6. **Safety-car toggle.** Inline "on: random SC/VSC periods" / "off: green-flag race", and a clearer explanation. The page keeps earlier optimizer runs for the same track and settings; if you ran it with the flag flipped, the summary adds "In your earlier run with them off, the best plan was ...". No new endpoint; it needs two runs by the user.
